Who we are

A family tradition of building

In Israel, the Soglowek name has been known for generations through the family’s food company. Uri Soglowek grew up inside that business and worked there until his military service. When he returned in 1997, his father introduced a new way of thinking: to manage the family’s wealth the way a family office would.

Show moreShow less

Uri and his siblings built that operation from one guiding principle: in business, you lose money where you don’t understand. It became the Soglowek Family Office, a single-family office run entirely in-house, investing in public companies and in commercial and residential real estate in Israel.

Learning America from the ground up

Uri’s early real estate investments in Israel and Europe did not deliver the returns the family expected. Rather than accept it, he moved to the United States and set out to learn the American market from the inside.

He joined a luxury residential developer in Florida and worked across the full life of a project, from land acquisition and permitting to delivery. His work included some of the state’s most recognized branded residences: the 29-story, 166-residence Roche Bobois Tower in St. Petersburg, the Aston Martin-branded tower in Daytona Beach Shores, and Serena by the Sea in Dunedin. The company was at the forefront of Florida’s branded-condominium movement beyond Miami.

Building his own platform

With that foundation, Uri went independent. He began with fix-and-flip projects funded entirely by family capital and completed dozens of them. Along the way he identified the market’s central challenge: the best deals rarely reach the open market. To solve it, his team built Sonar, a proprietary deal-sourcing platform now offered to the industry as software.

Today, the group invests across fix-and-flip, Section 8 rental housing, manufactured housing, and real estate lending. Until mid-2025, every investment was made with the Soglowek family’s own capital. Now, for the first time, the platform is opening to outside investors.

High-level investing. Boots-on-the-ground development.

How it started

Our own capital, then outside capital

Until mid-2025, all activity was the Soglowek family's own capital ("nostro"). Deal volume and the software then matured. Since mid-2025 we have opened the platform to outside capital: companies, family offices, and high-net-worth individuals.

Deal flow

What makes us unique

Sonar, software we built

Solving lead flow is the core of the model. Buying before a property hits the market means buying at a discount.

Sonar is proprietary software we built, a significant investment. It finds non-performing assets (NPA) and non-performing loans (NPL) across every county in the US. The product is also sold as SaaS to other flippers and wholesalers. Sonar can generate deals across every county in the US, depending on the client’s marketing budget. The average client pays about $7,000 and generates about $80,000 in wholesaling a month.

What we build

Five lines, one discipline

Fix-and-flip

Short holds

A typical flip returns about 25% on capital over 160 to 240 days. The hold is short, so exposure to interest rates is short. The flip company runs about 35 flips in parallel.

About 25% on capital · 160 to 240 days
Section 8 rentals

Packages of about 10 homes

Packages of about 10 homes in Columbus, Ohio, and in similar markets. Buy for about $50–60K, about $10K of rehab. Rent is about $1,300–$1,500 a month, paid by the federal voucher program.

ROI: 15-22% · About $50–60K buy · about $10K rehab · about $1,300–$1,500 rent
Manufactured housing

About 500 units, in preparation

Homes of about 60 to 230 square meters, with a premium finish. Uri puts the cost at about a third of conventional construction. After the infrastructure is in, a home is installed in about four days. The homes are factory-built, so the labor is the manufacturer's.

The project in preparation is the purchase of about 300 acres and the development of about 500 units, at an average cost of about $200,000 per unit.

Manufactured homes are about 6.5% of all US homes. Over 100,000 units are sold a year. It is a recession-proof model.

Multifamily

Buying as the market resets

We are building a unit to buy multifamily at a deep discount as the multifamily market resets. Investors who bought at 4.5–5 cap rates are now facing 7–8 cap rates.
